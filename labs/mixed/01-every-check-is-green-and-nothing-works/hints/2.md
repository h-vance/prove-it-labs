`docker compose ls` will tell you what is running and which files define it.
That is where the configuration lives.

Now separate two claims that people usually merge into one:

| Claim | What proves it | Who is asking |
|---|---|---|
| The application process is alive | The container's state | The platform |
| The application is serving requests | Its own health check | The container, from inside |
| A request from outside arrives | A request from outside | The customer |

The first two are green. You can confirm that in one command. The third is the
customer's ticket, and nobody has tested it yet.

So test it, and pay attention to *how* it fails. There is a real difference
between these, and each one points somewhere different:

- Nothing is listening at that address, so the connection is refused outright.
- Something accepted the connection and then gave you nothing back.
- Something answered, and the answer was an error.

Get the exact failure, not a summary of it. Then compare two numbers: the
address the customer is sent to, and the address the application says it is
listening on when it starts up. The application announces that in its very
first log line.
