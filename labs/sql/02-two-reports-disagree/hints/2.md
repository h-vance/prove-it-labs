Twelve is right. One hundred and eight is the inflated one, and the shape of
the inflation is the clue: enterprise is off by roughly nine times, and the
smaller plans by roughly two.

Ask what there are nine of per enterprise account, and two of per smaller one.

This is what happens when a query walks from one table to another where the
relationship is one-to-many. Every customer row becomes as many rows as it has
matches on the far side, and it becomes those rows **before** any aggregate
runs. By the time the count happens, the thing being counted is no longer
customers.

Run the query without its GROUP BY and look at the raw rows. What you see is
what the count is counting.
