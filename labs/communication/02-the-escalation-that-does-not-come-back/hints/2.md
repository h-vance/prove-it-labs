Six labels, because each one is a question the receiving engineer has to have
answered before they can pick this up:

| Label | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| Impact | Who is affected, how badly, since when, and who found it |
| Evidence | What you observed, quoted rather than summarized |
| Confirmed | What you established as working, so nobody re-checks it |
| Ruled out | What you eliminated, so nobody re-checks that either |
| Suspected cause | What you think it is, marked clearly as a belief |
| Request | The specific thing you want somebody to do |

`evidence.md` has the observations under `## Facts`, in the form they were
actually seen. Quote at least two of them verbatim. Paraphrasing an observation
turns it back into a conclusion, and then Priya is where she started.

The one people get wrong is **Request**. There are two separate asks available
in this incident and only one of them is obvious:

- The fix is already in, but the same edit may exist elsewhere. One
  misconfigured service is an incident; a shared template is the next outage.
- Nothing you own could see this. Every check ran inside the thing it was
  checking and passed for three hours while the service was unreachable.

The second is the one worth an engineer's week. The first is worth their
afternoon. Ask for both, in that order of importance, and say why.
