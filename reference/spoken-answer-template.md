# Spoken answer template

Technical rounds are not only a test of what you know. They test whether you
can be understood while you are thinking. Say this out loud. Reading it
silently does not build the same skill.

Target: 60 to 90 seconds.

---

## The shape

> I would first prove **[one operational fact]**.
>
> The first evidence I would check is **[command or query]**, because it tells
> me **[what it proves]**.
>
> If the output shows **[result]**, I know **[confirmed fact]**.
>
> If it does not, I would check **[alternate layer]** next.
>
> My safe next step would be **[read-only or reversible action]**.
>
> For the customer, I would say: **[plain-English update and next check-in]**.

## When you do not know

Saying this well is worth more than a confident wrong answer. Interviewers are
listening for whether you can narrow uncertainty rather than perform certainty.

> I would not want to overstate the root cause yet.
>
> I would narrow it by proving **[specific fact]** first.
>
> That tells me whether this is more likely **[layer A]** or **[layer B]**.
>
> Once I have that evidence, I can choose the least risky fix.

## Things that cost candidates offers

- Naming a fix before naming a proof.
- Presenting a hypothesis in the grammar of a fact.
- Reaching for a destructive or irreversible action in a production-flavored
  question when a read-only one would answer it.
- Explaining what a command does instead of what its output proved.
- Going silent while thinking. Narrate the search, not just the result.
